# Decision brief: appointment reminders

**Recommendation: retain email reminders; do not replace them with SMS.** Preserve the bounded reminder decision. Confidence is sufficient to defer replacement, but insufficient to claim email is universally superior.

The objective is reliable appointment reminders. This is a channel decision for the existing synthetic tool, not a company strategy redesign. The product owner remains accountable for changing it.

**Evidence ledger — source: supplied evaluation facts; observation period unspecified.**

| Evidence status | Statement | Decision implication |
|---|---|---|
| Supplied | Customers requested reminders. | Supports the reminder capability; does not establish SMS preference. |
| Supplied | Existing reminders work. | Supports retaining the current channel. “Work” has no supplied measurement definition. |
| Supplied | No evidence of channel failure or SMS willingness to pay. | Replacement lacks a demonstrated incremental benefit or commercial case. |
| Unknown | Email-related missed appointments, SMS preference, incremental outcome benefit and costs. | Could change the decision; absence of evidence does not prove absence of benefit. |

**Strategic X-Ray — causal-mechanism lens.** The replacement hypothesis is: email fails to reach recipients effectively, and SMS would improve appointment outcomes. The supplied working-reminder baseline is counterevidence to a presumed channel bottleneck. Reminder requests support the underlying need, not this replacement mechanism. No outcome or delivery evidence establishes that mechanism. This bounded examination confirms keeping email; it is not a completed empirical diagnosis of channel performance.

| Option | Strongest case | Trade-off and disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Retain email | Meets the supplied need through a working capability. | May miss an unobserved SMS-specific need; recommended now. |
| Replace email with SMS | Could improve reach where email demonstrably fails. | Would remove a working option while adding unproven migration and operating requirements; defer. |
| Offer optional SMS for an evidenced group | Could address a localized need while preserving email. | Adds channel management and cost; consider only if evidence emerges. |

**Economics and risk.** SMS must produce incremental customer value that justifies its incremental delivery, support and transition costs, with an explicit funding mechanism. Neither benefit nor cost is established. Willingness to pay could support funding, but is not mandatory if measured outcomes justify an approved subsidy. No prices, forecasts or thresholds are assumed. Keeping email avoids committing to an unsupported change and leaves the decision reversible.

**Next action and reversal gate.** Keep the current reminders operating; no new research or implementation is required to make this decision. Reopen it if traceable reports show email-related failures or a specific SMS need. The product owner should review affected customers’ reminder logs, support reports and appointment outcomes across their reported reminder-to-appointment event windows. Evidence of a channel-specific gap unlocks evaluation of optional SMS—not automatic replacement. Replacing email additionally requires comparative outcome and cost evidence and a viable transition for current recipients. Without that evidence, keep email and stop channel expansion.
