## Selection discipline

Select a framework only after naming the decision, lifecycle stage, product archetype, and evidence available. Use one primary spine and at most two supporting methods unless the user explicitly requests a workshop suite.

For every selected method, state its purpose, inputs, output, and limitation. A completed canvas is not proof that its contents are true.

## Strategy and positioning

| Decision | Prefer | Use when | Avoid or qualify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turn ambiguity into strategy | Strategy kernel: diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions | The organization has goals or initiatives but no causal choice | The diagnosis is merely a restated goal |
| Define competitive choices | Playing to Win choice cascade | Where-to-play/how-to-win choices and capabilities matter | Used as a fill-in exercise without market/customer evidence |
| Select focus areas | Product strategy: focus, insights, actions, management | Multiple promising problems compete for scarce resources | Leaders refuse to make exclusions |
| Position an offering | April Dunford positioning components | Alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value, best-fit customers, and category can be investigated | Treating positioning as a slogan or starting with a template statement |
| Understand progress sought | Jobs to Be Done | Switching behavior, context, motivation, and desired outcomes matter | Inventing job statements without interviews/behavioral evidence |
| Map customer value | Value Proposition Canvas | Customer jobs, pains, gains, products, relievers, and creators need alignment | Treating all pains as equally important or verified |
| Map the business system | Business Model Canvas | A new or changing model needs a shared system view | Using it instead of unit economics or competitive strategy |
| Assess industry structure | Five Forces | Supplier, buyer, substitute, entrant, and rivalry forces shape returns | Fast-changing category boundaries or no industry data |
| Assess durable advantage | Seven Powers / moat analysis | Scale economies, network economies, switching costs, branding, cornered resources, process power, or counter-positioning may exist | Calling any feature a moat; using without causal evidence |
| Explore growth direction | Ansoff matrix | Compare penetration, product development, market development, diversification | Treating four boxes as an answer rather than risk classes |

## Discovery and validation

| Decision | Prefer | Use when | Avoid or qualify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connect outcomes to customer opportunities | Opportunity Solution Tree | Teams are jumping from outcome to favorite solution | Opportunities are invented rather than derived from research |
| Identify riskiest beliefs | Assumption mapping | Desirability, viability, feasibility, usability, data, legal, or operational risk is uncertain | Scoring confidence without evidence definitions |
| Design a learning sequence | Riskiest-assumption test / Lean experiments | A reversible test can precede expensive build | A proxy cannot represent actual behavior or willingness to pay |
| Choose the evidence vehicle | Evidence-yield comparison + Full-Product Discovery | Build/rebuild is cheap, release is bounded and reversible, and integrated real behavior is decision-critical | Real release creates disproportionate safety, legal, privacy, financial, reputational, or operational exposure |
| Understand switching | JTBD switch interviews | Adoption/churn choices and competing alternatives matter | Leading questions or hypothetical future behavior dominate |
| Structure a complex journey | Journey/service blueprint | Multiple actors, channels, handoffs, or backstage operations shape value | The issue is a narrow one-step decision |
| Generate and test options | Dual-track discovery / product trio | Continuous learning should run alongside delivery | Used as a ceremony without decision rights or customer access |
| Define an MVP | Coherent narrow product or riskiest-assumption MVP | The smallest trustworthy learning or value system must be found | “MVP” means a low-quality fragment, or “full product” means the entire long-term roadmap |
| Start from desired customer experience | Working Backwards / PRFAQ | A new proposition needs customer-value clarity and internal alignment | Used as marketing copy without feasibility/economics checks |

Do not claim “validation” from compliments, survey intent alone, clicks without commitment, or a single customer. Match evidence strength to decision cost.

Do not make interviews, mockups, or pre-build research mandatory gates. Compare their evidence yield and total exposure with a bounded coherent product. Prefer the full product when it is equally cheap or cheaper to build and rebuild, only the integrated journey can reveal the mechanism, and real release remains limited and reversible.

## Business model, pricing, and growth

| Decision | Prefer | Use when | Avoid or qualify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Design a business model | Business Model Canvas + unit economics | Partners, channels, revenue, resources, activities, and costs interact | Canvas without cash, margins, adoption, or constraints |
| Test pricing architecture | Value metric + packaging hypotheses | Price must scale with perceived value and cost-to-serve | Copying competitor price without willingness-to-pay evidence |
| Estimate willingness to pay | Gabor-Granger, Van Westendorp, conjoint, or live offer tests | A research design and target sample can be defined | Reporting precise optimal price from tiny/biased samples |
| Size a market | Bottom-up + top-down triangulation + value theory | Market scope affects investment choice | Unsupported “X% of a huge market” SOM |
| Choose growth mechanism | Funnel + loops + channel economics | Acquisition, activation, retention, referral, expansion mechanisms can be measured | Growth strategy before retention/value is credible |
| Diagnose product-market fit | Cohort retention, usage depth, pull, repeat purchase, sales efficiency, qualitative evidence; PMF survey as one input | Multiple evidence streams exist | A single threshold or NPS is treated as proof |
| Design a metric system | North Star + input metrics + guardrails | Customer value delivery can be measured and decomposed | A revenue metric masquerades as customer value, or one metric applies to every model |

## Prioritization, roadmaps, and execution

| Decision | Prefer | Use when | Avoid or qualify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rank comparable initiatives | RICE | Reach, impact, confidence, and effort share a defined period and scale | Strategy, dependencies, compliance, or table stakes dominate; inputs are invented |
| Prioritize by delay economics | WSJF / cost of delay | Relative cost of delay and job size can be estimated consistently | Pseudo-precision or incomparable work types |
| Separate satisfaction effects | Kano research | Users can evaluate functional/dysfunctional pairs with suitable sampling | Team guesses categories without research |
| Scope commitments | MoSCoW | A fixed delivery boundary and decision authority exist | Used to rank a long-term strategy backlog |
| Balance risk and reward | Value-effort-risk matrix | Early comparison needs a transparent qualitative view | Scores hide different assumptions or units |
| Build a roadmap | Outcome/problem roadmap | Strategy is set but solutions remain hypotheses | Fixed feature/date commitments are presented as discovery |
| Shape bounded bets | Shape Up appetite/betting concepts | Teams can work to fixed appetite with variable scope | Hard regulatory scope or tightly coupled dependencies make shaping insufficient |
| Translate strategy into goals | OKRs | Outcomes and measurable results can align teams | Key results are task lists or targets without causal logic |

Use prioritization scores as decision support, not automatic decisions. Always surface dependencies, mandates, strategic fit, reversibility, and confidence.

## Metrics, portfolio, and organization

| Decision | Prefer | Use when | Avoid or qualify when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build a metric tree | North Star + input metrics + guardrails | A customer-value event and causal inputs exist | Vanity activity or revenue alone is selected |
| Allocate across horizons | Three Horizons | Core, emerging, and future bets compete for resources | Horizons are treated as fixed calendar buckets |
| Decide product posture | Invest / maintain / transform / partner / sunset | Portfolio-level allocation and exit choices are needed | A label is assigned without trajectory, economics, strategic role, and transition cost |
| Compare portfolio roles | BCG-like share/growth lens | Reliable market-share and category-growth data exist | New categories, platform synergies, or missing share data |
| Map value-chain evolution | Wardley Mapping | User need, value chain, evolution, and climatic patterns can be credibly mapped | A decorative landscape is produced without operational knowledge |
| Design product organization | Product operating model + Team Topologies | Strategy, team missions, dependencies, and enabling/platform needs must align | Copying ratios or org charts from another company |
| Plan under external uncertainty | Scenario planning + signposts | Multiple plausible futures would change today’s choices | Scenarios are presented as forecasts or assigned arbitrary probabilities |

## Product-archetype adjustments

### B2B SaaS and enterprise

Separate user, champion, buyer, approver, security, procurement, and administrator. Model sales cycle, implementation, expansion, support, gross retention, net retention, and concentration. Do not substitute user engagement for account value.

### Consumer subscription

Emphasize acquisition source, activation, cohort retention, usage frequency, renewal, refunds, store fees, and content/service cost. Avoid blended averages that hide cohort decay.

### Marketplace

Analyze each side separately: acquisition, activation, liquidity, match quality, time to match, repeat rate, disintermediation, trust/safety, incentives, and local network density. GMV is not revenue and revenue is not contribution.

### Platform, API, data, and AI

Map developer/user adoption, integration cost, reliability, governance, ecosystem incentives, variable compute/tool cost, human review, data rights, quality evaluation, safety, and switching costs. Treat model quality claims as testable, task-specific hypotheses.

### Physical product and hardware

Include bill of materials, yield, freight, duties, inventory, working capital, returns, warranty, channel margin, service, compliance, and production constraints. Distinguish sell-in from sell-through.

### Services

Model utilization, realization, delivery capacity, senior-talent bottlenecks, rework, scope creep, standardization, and founder dependency. A service may be productized without becoming software.

For founder- or expert-led services, make the capacity equation explicit before proposing products: realized revenue is constrained by available expert time, utilization, realized price, and delivery leverage. Compare price/selectivity, standardized delivery, asynchronous IP products, augmented expert systems, expert networks, and client-embedded capability as different operating models—not as features in one backlog. Measure how each changes expert hours per outcome, quality variance, willingness to pay, recurrence, marginal cost, key-person risk, and defensibility.

### Regulated or safety-critical products

Treat legal, clinical, security, privacy, model-risk, accessibility, and certification requirements as design inputs. Do not make legal, medical, or compliance conclusions without qualified evidence.

### Internal or mission products

Replace revenue-only logic with measurable mission value, risk reduction, cycle time, quality, adoption, avoided cost, or capability creation while still accounting for total cost and opportunity cost.
