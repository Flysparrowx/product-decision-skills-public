# Source Intelligence Board

Use this method when recurring channels, publications, experts, communities, datasets, or research feeds are intended to shape product imagination, venture-studio intake, portfolio choices, or business transformation.

## Purpose

Build a cognitive portfolio, not a larger feed. Assign sources different decision roles so they challenge and complete one another. Treat commentary as a hypothesis generator unless it links to stronger evidence.

The board should help answer:

- what may be changing in customer behavior, technology, distribution, economics, or regulation;
- where working products, failed attempts, or credible counterexamples already exist;
- what the signal could mean for the product or portfolio;
- what direct evidence is still required before committing.

## Required lenses

Cover only lenses relevant to the decision, but avoid relying on the idea-generation lens alone:

| Lens | Contribution | Typical bias to expose |
|---|---|---|
| Product imagination | New behaviors, scenarios, combinations, and opportunity prompts | Novelty, rhetoric, survivorship |
| Real launches | Products actually shipped, buyers reached, and post-launch trajectories | Launch selection and promotional framing |
| Customer and behavior | Direct observations, communities, support, search, usage, transactions | Sample and channel bias |
| Operator craft | Product, GTM, growth, delivery, and organizational mechanisms | Context loss and framework evangelism |
| Economics and capital | Pricing, margins, unit economics, funding, market structure | Investor incentives and power-law thinking |
| Failure and critique | Closures, weak retention, failed channels, unintended effects, counterarguments | Negativity and hindsight bias |
| Primary evidence | Company data, filings, official statistics, regulation, research, telemetry | Narrow scope, lag, or measurement limits |

Do not force every board to have one source per row. One strong source may serve several lenses, but record the overlap and avoid counting it as independent corroboration.

## SourceBoard contract

For each source record:

| Field | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Source and owner | Identify the author, organization, or data producer |
| Decision role | State which lens and decisions it informs |
| Expected signal | Describe what useful observation should emerge |
| Provenance | Identify primary reporting, aggregation, reposting, or shared upstream sources |
| Incentives and bias | Note sales, investment, audience, ideology, geography, or survivorship effects |
| Freshness and cadence | Record relevant period and review frequency |
| Evidence status | Label inspiration, secondary evidence, or primary evidence |
| Contradiction partner | Name the lens or source expected to challenge it |
| Keep/replace rule | Define when repetition, staleness, low signal, or conflict of interest removes it |

Prefer one or two high-signal sources per lens over many overlapping feeds. Popularity and posting frequency are not quality measures.

## Workflow

1. Name the decisions the board should improve. Do not build a generic trend digest.
2. Map the required lenses and identify blind spots.
3. Select sources with distinct owners, incentives, geographies, and evidence types where relevant.
4. Trace shared provenance. Deduplicate sources that repeat the same original claim.
5. Sample a decision-relevant period before committing to a recurring feed.
6. Capture signals with source, date, underlying event period, lens, contradiction, confidence, and possible product implication.
7. Cluster repeated signals without turning repetition into truth.
8. Convert each consequential signal into a falsifiable hypothesis, required direct evidence, and a decision it could change.
9. Route the hypothesis into discovery, a bounded live-product test, strategy red-team, or monitoring. Never route it directly into a roadmap because it was compelling.
10. Review the board periodically; remove redundant or low-yield sources and fill newly visible blind spots.

## Signal-to-decision chain

Use this structure:

```text
Signal
→ provenance and incentives
→ independent confirmation or contradiction
→ product/business implication
→ falsifiable hypothesis
→ direct evidence or credible test
→ CONTINUE | NARROW | REDESIGN | PARTNER | KILL | MONITOR
```

When build cost is low and the actual end-to-end experience is necessary to observe behavior, the credible test may be a bounded full-product release. Keep audience, operational exposure, and irreversible commitments limited; instrument the product and precommit the decision rule. Do not demand a weaker proxy merely because it looks more like traditional discovery.

## Output

Return the smallest useful combination of:

- a SourceBoard showing roles, biases, provenance, contradictions, and cadence;
- a signal map with repeated themes and meaningful disagreements;
- a hypothesis backlog linked to product or portfolio decisions;
- explicit blind spots and evidence requests;
- recommendations to keep, replace, demote, or add source roles.

Keep summaries subordinate to decisions. The output is not a reading report, popularity ranking, or claim that the sources collectively prove demand.

## Completion gate

Before using the board in a recommendation, verify:

- no single author or content network dominates the thesis;
- apparent corroboration is not shared provenance or reposting;
- source incentives and material biases are visible;
- failures and contradictions survived synthesis;
- inspiration, secondary evidence, and primary evidence remain distinct;
- every consequential signal is connected to a falsifiable hypothesis or monitoring question;
- channel volume did not substitute for customer behavior, economics, or direct evidence.
