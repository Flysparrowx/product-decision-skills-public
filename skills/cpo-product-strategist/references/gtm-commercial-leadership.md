## 1. Commercial system

Connect product strategy to a repeatable path from qualified demand to retained value and contribution:

```text
Target and trigger
→ promise and proof
→ route to market
→ purchase/approval
→ implementation and first value
→ habitual or organizational value
→ renewal, expansion, or repeat
→ contribution and learning
```

GTM is not a launch campaign. Product, marketing, sales, customer success, operations, finance, security, legal, partners, and support must share the same target, promise boundary, evidence, economics, and feedback loop.

## 2. ICP and buying architecture

Define segment and explicit non-targets using problem intensity, trigger, current alternative, ability to reach and serve, willingness/authority to pay, implementation fit, risk, retention potential, and contribution. Separate user, champion, economic buyer, approver, procurement, security, legal, administrator, beneficiary, and blocker.

For every segment state the job, switching event, proof required, objections, buying process, sales/implementation burden, value realization period, and expansion path. Do not treat an enthusiastic user as a qualified enterprise opportunity.

## 3. Packaging, pricing, and sales motion

Choose self-serve, product-led sales, sales-led, channel, marketplace, embedded, partnership, or hybrid motion from deal complexity, risk, ACV, customization, buyer education, procurement, and support requirements.

Align value metric, package fences, contract unit, usage/overage, service level, implementation, support, and promise boundary. Model realized price, discount authority, channel share, sales capacity, cost-to-serve, and gross margin. Prevent custom deals from silently creating permanent product or service obligations.

## 4. Pilot and launch readiness

A pilot must test a commercial and value hypothesis, not provide indefinite free consulting. Define target account, buyer commitment, bounded use case, baseline, success/stop criteria, data and integration requirements, price or conversion condition, owner, timeline when supported, and path to production.

Launch gate:

| Area | Required readiness |
|---|---|
| Product | target job, quality bar, limitations, instrumentation, rollback |
| Commercial | ICP, positioning, package, price, contract, forecast treatment |
| Sales | qualification, demo/proof, objections, promise rules, enablement |
| Delivery | onboarding, implementation, migration, capacity, support and escalation |
| Trust | security, privacy, legal, accessibility, compliance and claims review |
| Measurement | activation, time-to-value, adoption, retention, contribution and feedback |

Use staged availability when evidence, capacity, risk, or support is not ready for a broad launch.

## 5. Adoption, expansion, and renewal

Map contracted value to realized value. For B2B distinguish contract signature, technical implementation, administrator readiness, user activation, workflow adoption, organizational outcome, renewal, and expansion. Assign ownership at each transition.

Track time and loss between stages, usage depth only where it represents value, stakeholder health, support burden, value proof, renewal risk, and expansion mechanism. For consumer and transactional products adapt to first value, repeat, habit/natural cycle, reactivation, and referral.

Customer Success should not compensate indefinitely for missing product capability or unclear implementation. Product should not optimize engagement while customers fail to realize the purchased outcome.

## 6. Commercial feedback and metrics

Create a common evidence loop from CRM, product telemetry, billing, support, research, implementation, and finance. Reconcile definitions and cohorts.

Useful measures include qualified pipeline by ICP, stage conversion and age, win/loss reason quality, realized price and discount, sales cycle, pilot-to-paid, implementation completion, time-to-value, activation, retained value, GRR/NRR or repeat, expansion, support and services intensity, CAC/payback, contribution, and concentration.

Do not reward pipeline volume without qualification, bookings without collectability/delivery, adoption without value, NRR without cohort/mix decomposition, or win rates distorted by stage definitions. Review lost, stalled, churned, and unprofitable customers, not only wins.

Maintain a promise ledger for material commitments made in sales, contracts, launch communications, and partner agreements. Route new commitments through product, engineering, finance, security/legal, and delivery owners as applicable.

Treat `promise debt` as the accumulated gap between customer-facing commitments and funded, feasible, governed delivery capability. Diagnose its source: custom deal, ambiguous contract, weak qualification, missing product capability, unpriced service work, dependency, capacity, or quality/risk gate. Measure affected customers/revenue, delivery and support load, lateness/exposure, margin impact, and recurrence. Resolve through delivery, renegotiation, productization, pricing/service change, or explicit discontinuation; do not silently force the roadmap to absorb it.

## 7. Partnerships and internationalization

For a partnership define joint customer outcome, contribution by each party, distribution/integration mechanism, economics, data/IP, service ownership, dependency, exclusivity, termination, and measurement. A logo or memorandum is not a product channel.

For international expansion test segment demand, local alternatives, language and cultural workflow, pricing and payment, tax/currency, data residency, regulation, support hours, contracts, channel, local implementation, and unit economics. Translate the value mechanism and operating model, not only interface copy.

## 8. Deliverable

Provide ICP/non-targets, buying committee and journey, positioning/proof, package and price logic, sales/channel motion, pilot design, launch gate, adoption/renewal system, commercial metric tree, ownership, economics, risks, and next evidence gates. Leave unsupported conversion, price, cycle, quota, and timeline inputs symbolic.
