## Universal output rules

Lead with the conclusion. Match depth to the decision. Do not fill sections with generic prose; omit or mark Unknown when evidence is absent.

Every consequential deliverable should expose:

- decision and objective;
- evidence and assumptions;
- options and trade-offs;
- economics or mission-value mechanism;
- recommendation and confidence;
- risks, dependencies, reversibility, and stop conditions;
- next actions and learning plan.

Before accepting a consequential strategy, steelman and red-team its 3–5 load-bearing assumptions. For each, specify `Fails if`, evidence to obtain, cheapest credible test, and kill/change criterion.

When several unknowns compete for research, add a compact `Decision Sensitivity Map`:

| Unknown or load-bearing assumption | Fails if | Decision consequence | Evidence needed | Cheapest credible test |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Include only unknowns whose plausible resolution would change, stop, narrow, or defer the current decision. Name non-decision-changing unknowns as `Do not research now`; do not create research merely to complete the table.

## Decision brief

Use for a single executive choice.

1. Decision required
2. Recommendation
3. Why now
4. Evidence ledger
5. Options table
6. Economics and sensitivity
7. Risks and reversibility
8. Decision Sensitivity Map — what evidence would change, stop, narrow, or defer the recommendation
9. Next decision/action

## Full product strategy

1. Executive decision summary
2. Scope, horizon, objective, and constraints
3. Product archetype and lifecycle stage
4. Evidence base and material unknowns
5. Diagnosis / strategic challenge
6. Target user, buyer, payer, job, and segment priority
7. Competitive alternatives and positioning
8. Where to play / how to win
9. Value proposition and business model
10. Strategic options considered
11. Chosen guiding policy and coherent bets
12. Explicit non-goals and trade-offs
13. Economics, market sizing, and scenarios
14. Capabilities, partners, operating model, and dependencies
15. Metric tree and guardrails
16. Outcome roadmap and experiment portfolio
17. Risks, signposts, kill/continue criteria
18. Immediate decisions and an evidence-gated sequence; add dates only when supported by deadline, capacity, dependencies, or observed cycle time

## Strategy on a page

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Objective | Measurable outcome and horizon |
| Diagnosis | The pivotal challenge and evidence |
| Target | Priority segment/job and explicit non-target |
| Position | Alternative, differentiated value, category/context |
| Where to play | Market, use case, channel, geography, layer |
| How to win | Advantage mechanism and required capabilities |
| Guiding policy | Chosen approach and trade-offs |
| Bets | 3–5 coherent actions/hypotheses |
| Economics | Contribution mechanism and dominant drivers |
| Measures | North Star/outcome, inputs, guardrails |
| Unknowns | Decision-critical assumptions and tests |
| Stop conditions | Evidence that triggers change or exit |

## Discovery and experiment plan

For each assumption:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Assumption | Specific, falsifiable belief |
| Risk class | Value, viability, feasibility, usability, data, legal, operational, go-to-market |
| Current evidence | Source and confidence |
| Decision affected | What changes if false |
| Test | Smallest credible test |
| Population/sample | Who/what and why |
| Signal | Observable behavior or result |
| Threshold | Precommitted continue/change/stop rule |
| Bias/risk | Main validity threat |
| Owner/review | Accountability and decision point |

Prioritize by decision impact × uncertainty × cost of being wrong, not by ease alone.

## Economics and scenario pack

1. Model boundary and contribution definition
2. Input dictionary with provenance
3. Product Resource Ledger — direct, shared, allocated, and incremental resources
4. Current Product Performance Scorecard — customer value, product metrics, unit/cohort economics, quality, capacity, and evidence maturity
5. Lifecycle value and potential — current run-rate, evidence-bounded upside, and option upside
6. Unit economics by segment/cohort
7. Capacity, opportunity cost, and cash constraints
8. Product P&L or Hypothesis Business Case / Shadow P&L as appropriate
9. Base/downside/upside drivers only when anchored
10. Sensitivity ranking
11. Break-even conditions
12. Kill/continue/invest thresholds
13. Decision calculator — editable inputs, locked formulas, units, provenance, decision boundary, uncertainty, and normal/boundary/invalid checks
14. Missing data, instrumentation, and promotion gate from hypothesis to forecast/budget

Use the smallest useful calculator: inline formula/table, then spreadsheet-style model, then a minimal read-only interactive surface only when nonlinear or multi-scenario exploration changes the decision.

## Outcome roadmap

| Horizon/review point | Outcome/problem | Strategic bet | Evidence/test | Metric + guardrail | Dependency | Owner | Stop/change trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use horizons or review points instead of unsupported exact dates. Do not default to 30/60/90 days. Keep solutions provisional when discovery remains open.

## Portfolio review

For each product or bet:

- strategic role and customer/job;
- lifecycle and trajectory;
- revenue, contribution, cash, and capacity use;
- retention/repeat and evidence of pull;
- differentiation, synergy, and option value;
- risk and transition cost;
- posture: invest, maintain, transform, partner, harvest, or sunset;
- trigger for posture change.

Then show portfolio concentration, resource allocation, dependencies, gaps, cannibalization, and explicit reallocation decisions. Do not use a single 2×2 as the sole decision basis.

## Board product memo

1. Decisions/asks
2. Customer and market change
3. Product outcomes versus plan
4. Cohort/portfolio economics
5. Strategic bets and evidence
6. Risks, signposts, and mitigations
7. Resource implications
8. Next board-level decision

Avoid feature inventories. Explain causal movement in outcomes and economics.

## Strategy audit

Score qualitatively and explain evidence for:

- diagnosis clarity;
- customer/buyer evidence;
- choice and focus;
- differentiation and advantage;
- business-model coherence;
- economic viability;
- metric quality;
- learning system;
- roadmap coherence;
- capability/operating-model fit;
- uncertainty handling;
- stop conditions.

Return: strengths, unsupported claims, contradictions, missing decisions, highest-risk assumptions, and the smallest repair plan.
