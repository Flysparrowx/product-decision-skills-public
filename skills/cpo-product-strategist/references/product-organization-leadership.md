## 1. Organization diagnosis

Design the organization from strategy and work, not an org-chart fashion. Establish:

- company and product stage, portfolio, strategic bets, and non-goals;
- value streams, customer segments, product/platform boundaries, and critical capabilities;
- current teams, roles, reporting lines, spans, layers, locations, and contractors;
- decision latency, handoffs, dependencies, overloaded roles, shadow ownership, and recurring conflict;
- capacity allocation, skill gaps, leadership bottlenecks, and key-person risk;
- incentives, review cadence, and where evidence fails to change decisions.

Distinguish structural, capability, process, leadership, incentive, and strategy problems. A reorg cannot repair unclear strategy, weak managers, missing skills, or contradictory incentives by itself.

## 2. Team topology and capacity

Prefer durable ownership of a customer outcome, value stream, platform, enabling capability, or genuinely complicated subsystem. Choose topology from cognitive load, coupling, risk, and required speed. Do not create a platform team without internal customers, a mandate, adoption path, and service measures.

For each team define mission, users, outcomes, scope, exclusions, decision rights, interfaces, dependencies, required skills, capacity, and health measures. Product, design, engineering, data, research, operations, marketing, and domain expertise need not report to one executive to operate as one accountable system.

Build capacity bottom-up:

```text
Available change capacity
= staffed capacity
- run/support/on-call load
- mandatory risk/compliance work
- known leave and vacancy
- coordination and transition cost
```

Allocate capacity to strategic bets, core health, obligations, platform/enabling work, and options. Expose unfunded strategy instead of pretending every priority fits. Headcount requests must state outcome, bottleneck, alternative, ramp time, loaded cost, and condition for review.

## 3. Roles and decision rights

Write role charters around decisions and outcomes, not task inventories. For every recurring consequential decision name one accountable owner, required contributors, decision input, escalation path, and review trigger.

Avoid consensus vetoes, duplicate product ownership, PMs acting only as coordinators, engineering reduced to estimates, design treated as decoration, or executives bypassing team ownership through private commitments. Use a product trio/quartet only when it clarifies collaboration; do not turn it into a universal reporting structure.

## 4. Hiring and talent systems

Before opening a role, define the problem the hire must solve, decisions they will own, stage/archetype context, evidence of success, and capabilities required now versus learnable later.

Use a structured scorecard and consistent evidence:

- relevant outcomes and scale, with the candidate's actual contribution;
- product judgment, customer/economic reasoning, execution, collaboration, and learning;
- leadership scope, team development, conflict, and difficult decisions for manager roles;
- work sample or case tied to the real role, with explicit evaluation criteria;
- references checked against load-bearing claims where lawful and appropriate.

Do not select on charisma, pedigree, framework vocabulary, unpaid excessive assignments, or undefined `culture fit`. Use inclusive, job-related criteria and comply with applicable employment law and company policy.

## 5. Performance, coaching, and careers

Evaluate a balanced record:

- outcomes and decision quality under the available evidence;
- product craft and customer understanding;
- economic, quality, risk, and operational judgment;
- collaboration, team leverage, and talent development;
- learning, correction, and ownership of failures.

Do not rate PMs on feature volume, roadmap punctuality without context, stakeholder happiness, hours, or metric movement they did not causally control. Separate outcome, behavior, skill, scope, and potential. Calibrate across comparable roles and preserve evidence.

Coaching should identify one or two observable capability gaps, practice opportunities, feedback cadence, support, and evidence of improvement. Career levels should distinguish increasing complexity, ambiguity, influence, scope, and talent leverage rather than tenure or title inflation.

For underperformance, clarify expectations, evidence, context, support, and review points. Any formal employment action requires the employer's HR process, documentation standard, legal rules, and accountable manager; do not present generic advice as a lawful procedure.

## 6. Executive influence and conflict

Map the decision system:

| Actor | Decision authority | Incentive/fear | Evidence needed | Commitment required |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Pre-wire consequential decisions with required experts and affected owners without turning private alignment into a hidden decision. Frame conflict as competing objectives, assumptions, constraints, or risk tolerances. Make trade-offs, reversibility, and decision rights explicit.

Escalate when local teams cannot resolve a cross-company trade-off, authority is ambiguous, a commitment exceeds capacity, or legal/safety/financial exposure requires executive ownership. Record the decision, rationale, dissent, owner, and revisit trigger. Do not optimize for harmony at the expense of a real decision.

## 7. Reorganization and change

Use a change hypothesis:

```text
Current mechanism
→ observed cost or blocked outcome
→ target capability
→ structural/process/incentive change
→ transition evidence
```

Specify what changes and what does not; roles and reporting; decision migration; product and operational continuity; talent and knowledge risks; temporary capacity loss; communication by audience; consultation requirements; and 30/60/90-day dates only when the organization supplies a real timeline.

Sequence reversible pilots when possible. Define leading indicators such as decision latency, dependency load, role clarity, team health, hiring/ramp, and customer/reliability continuity. Do not declare success because the new org chart launched.

## 8. Deliverable and guardrails

Provide current-state diagnosis, design principles, target topology, role/decision map, capacity and headcount logic, talent-system changes, transition plan, measures, risks, and unresolved executive decisions.

Never invent employee performance, motives, compensation, headcount affordability, legal obligations, or confidential sentiment. A skill can design and stress-test the operating system; accountable leaders must make people decisions and conduct real conversations.
