# Source Playbook

Use this guide to plan lawful, platform-aware evidence collection. Re-check current platform rules and local law at execution time.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when relevant:

1. Consented first-party evidence: interviews, support logs, research diaries, product analytics, sales conversations.
2. Primary public group expression: public posts, comments, forums, creator content, event participation, public community artifacts.
3. Observable behavior: schedules, reviews, event cadence, public transactions/offerings, search-interest data, recurring workflows.
4. Institutional and place context: venue pages, associations, public statistics, maps, local media, regulations, public calendars.
5. Secondary interpretation: journalism, agency reports, trend decks, analyst summaries.

Use secondary sources to discover vocabulary and hypotheses. Validate important claims against primary evidence.

## Platform-aware collection

- Prefer official APIs, platform search, approved connectors, exports supplied by the user, and normal public browsing.
- Record query, filters, date range, locale, sort order, and access method.
- Note recommendation/search ranking effects; “top” results are not a neutral sample.
- Do not bypass logins, robots controls, paywalls, rate limits, deleted-content boundaries, or closed-group access.
- Paraphrase by default. Use short quotes only when exact language is analytically necessary and safe.
- Avoid storing usernames, profile URLs, photos, or raw posts when an aggregated code or evidence reference is enough.

## Query expansion loop

Start with five seed classes:

```text
self-labels and role names
places, venues, routes, and events
problems, aspirations, and repeated questions
rituals, tools, products, and organizations
phrases, jokes, hashtags, spelling variants, and transliterations
```

For each strong signal, search:

```text
exact phrase
phrase + place
phrase + activity/role
phrase + complaint/problem
phrase + event/season
phrase on a second platform
opposite or skeptical phrasing
```

Maintain a query log. Stop expanding terms that only reproduce the same platform cluster.

## Group-specific source mixes

### Place-bound microgroup

Examples: people using one sports hall, neighborhood parents, vendors around one market.

- public venue materials, schedule, pricing, rules, events;
- map/review ecosystem and nearby complementary services;
- local public groups, location tags, event pages, local media;
- time/route/friction observations at aggregate level;
- consented interviews with adult members, organizers, staff, or guardians;
- comparison with one nearby venue or analogous locality.

If minors are present, exclude profile enumeration, direct outreach, identifiable quotes, face analysis, and personal routine mapping. Assign an adult research owner; use guardian consent and participant assent for direct research where applicable; minimize raw data; restrict access; set a short retention/deletion period; and use only adult public professionals or organizational accounts as named exemplars.

### Professional/B2B microvertical

- professional associations and standards;
- job posts and workflow/tool discussions;
- webinars, conference programs, public expert posts;
- software/service reviews and implementation complaints;
- buyer, user, approver, and blocker interviews;
- procurement, compliance, budget-cycle, and seasonality context.

Do not equate public thought leaders with ordinary practitioners.

### Interest, fandom, or creator microculture

- public community spaces and comments;
- recurring formats, memes, remixes, symbols, and lore;
- events, merchandise, fan labor, moderator rules;
- creator collaborations and audience overlap;
- newcomers, lapsed members, critics, and adjacent fandoms.

Separate community-native expression from brand-seeded promotion.

### Local business ecosystem

- public business listings, reviews, menus/catalogs, schedules;
- owner/operator communities and trade chats where access is authorized;
- local events, chambers, suppliers, job posts, and regulations;
- public customer questions and complaints;
- operator interviews and observational walkthroughs.

Separate business-owner needs from end-customer needs.

## Geo-social evidence

Use place at the least precise level required for the decision:

- city/region for market sizing and trend comparison;
- neighborhood/catchment for access and local habits;
- venue for service design when authorized;
- never home/live/individual routine precision.

Treat geotagged data as sparse and biased. Validate place claims using schedules, maps, reviews, events, interviews, or observation. A location tag may describe content, not the author's actual location.

## Network and lookalike expansion

Use a graph only when relationships matter. Possible nodes: public organizations, creators, venues, events, topics, tools, and content artifacts. Possible edges: collaboration, mention, reply, co-attendance, shared event, or shared topic.

Expand from a seed through:

- repeated collaborators and public organizers;
- shared events, venues, tools, and media;
- recurring cross-posted phrases or formats;
- adjacent clusters with similar jobs/tensions but different identity markers;
- bridge nodes connecting otherwise separate clusters.

Validate candidates manually. High graph proximity can reflect controversy, spam, or algorithmic promotion rather than cultural similarity.

## Bias checklist

Check for:

- platform and algorithmic visibility bias;
- loud-minority and creator dominance;
- deleted, private, offline, or non-posting members;
- bot, coordinated, sponsored, and reposted content;
- language, literacy, access, and device bias;
- tourist/visitor signals mistaken for local behavior;
- seasonal, event-driven, and news-driven spikes;
- researcher confirmation and translation bias;
- overrepresentation of extreme reviews;
- cross-platform identity assumptions.
