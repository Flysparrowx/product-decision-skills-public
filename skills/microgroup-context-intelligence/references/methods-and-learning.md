# Methods and Learning Guide

## Method stack

Use a mixed-method stack rather than treating social listening as sufficient:

1. **Netnography** for systematic entry, immersion, collection, interpretation, ethics, and representation in online cultures.
2. **Digital ethnography** for the entanglement of online/offline life, places, objects, devices, routines, and researcher reflexivity.
3. **Thematic analysis** for an explicit codebook, themes, counterexamples, and revision.
4. **Social network analysis** for clusters, bridge nodes, centralization, and audience adjacency when relational data exists.
5. **Geo-social analysis** for place, mobility, temporal patterns, local institutions, and online/offline coupling.
6. **Product/customer research** for jobs, desired progress, alternatives, frictions, and behavior validation.
7. **Trend analysis** for velocity, persistence, spread, and baseline comparison.

LLMs may help retrieve, translate, code, cluster, and summarize. They must not fabricate observations, replace consent, infer sensitive traits, or become the sole judge of validity.

## Core books and methods

- Robert V. Kozinets, *Netnography: The Essential Guide to Qualitative Social Media Research*, 3rd ed., SAGE, 2019. Core method for online cultures and communities: https://uk.sagepub.com/en-gb/eur/netnography/book260905
- Sarah Pink, Heather Horst, John Postill, Larissa Hjorth, Tania Lewis, Jo Tacchi, *Digital Ethnography: Principles and Practice*, SAGE, 2016. Online/offline, locality, objects, events, and lived practice: https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/digital-ethnography-principles-and-practice/
- Martyn Hammersley and Paul Atkinson, *Ethnography: Principles in Practice*, 4th ed., Routledge, 2019. Sampling, access, field relations, reflexivity, interviewing, and analysis: https://www.routledge.com/Ethnography-Principles-in-Practice/Hammersley-Atkinson/p/book/9781138504455
- Virginia Braun and Victoria Clarke, *Thematic Analysis: A Practical Guide*, SAGE, 2021. Use for disciplined familiarization, coding, theme development, review, naming, and reporting.
- Albert-László Barabási, *Network Science*. Free online foundation for network structure and communities: https://networksciencebook.com/
- Greg Guest, Arwen Bunce, Laura Johnson, “How Many Interviews Are Enough?”, *Field Methods* 18(1), 2006. Treat saturation as an empirical stopping concept, not a magic sample-size rule: https://doi.org/10.1177/1525822X05279903

## Ethics and privacy standards

- Association of Internet Researchers, *Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0*. Use contextual, lifecycle-wide ethical judgment rather than “public means free to use”: https://aoir.org/ethics/
- ESOMAR/GRBN Online Research Guideline. Use for voluntary participation, disclosure, passive collection, sample management, security, and separation of research from direct marketing: https://community.esomar.org/what-we-do/code-guidelines/esomargrbn-online-research-guideline
- NIST Privacy Framework. Use data minimization, governance, risk assessment, and purpose limitation: https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework/privacy-framework

Always check current local law, platform terms, contractual limits, and organizational policies. This skill is a research workflow, not legal advice.

## Geo-social interpretation cautions

Geotagged social data can reveal spatial and temporal patterns, but it is sparse, platform-specific, and demographically biased. Do not infer the population from posters. Combine it with place evidence and first-party validation.

- George Mason University GGS 681 describes the relevant intersection: geospatial social media collection, location-based and cyberspace network analysis, content analysis, and geovisualization: https://cehd.gmu.edu/courses/GGS-681
- “Social Media Data: Challenges, Opportunities and Limitations in Urban Studies” reviews platform and representativeness limitations across location-based sources: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2018.11.001
- “Understanding Public Opinions from Geosocial Media” discusses low geotag coverage and uneven geographic representativeness: https://www.mdpi.com/2220-9964/5/6/74

## Trend interpretation

Google Trends is aggregated, anonymized, sampled, normalized search interest. It is not absolute volume, a poll, or evidence that a microgroup holds an opinion. Low-volume local terms may be suppressed or noisy.

- Google Trends data FAQ: https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533
- Google News Initiative training on interpreting Trends: https://newsinitiative.withgoogle.com/resources/trainings/fundamentals/google-trends-understanding-the-data/

Use trends as one triangulated signal. Compare time windows, locations, related terms, platform-native behavior, and qualitative evidence.

## Suggested learning path

1. Start with the free DARIAH Netnography module for method orientation: https://campus.dariah.eu/resources/external/netnography
2. Study Kozinets for a complete netnographic workflow and immersion journal practice.
3. Study Pink et al. to connect digital traces with places, bodies, routines, and offline life.
4. Learn thematic analysis and practice building an auditable codebook from a small corpus.
5. Learn network basics and community detection through Barabási or the University of California/Coursera Social Network Analysis course: https://www.coursera.org/learn/social-network-analysis
6. Add geo-social methods through a course such as George Mason GGS 681 or an equivalent GIS/social-media-analysis syllabus.
7. Study AoIR and ESOMAR ethics before collecting from real communities.
8. Run a bounded pilot, compare the pack with interviews from group members, and convert corrections into regression cases.

Current university examples for deeper study include Oxford’s Digital Ethnography course and Cambridge Research Methods’ Digital Ethnography module:

- https://lifelong-learning.ox.ac.uk/courses/digital-ethnography
- https://www.training.cam.ac.uk/jsss/course/carm-digeth

Course availability changes; treat these as curriculum references and verify current dates before recommending enrollment.
