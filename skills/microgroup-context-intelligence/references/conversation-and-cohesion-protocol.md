# Conversation and Cohesion Protocol

Use this protocol when the task asks how people communicate, whether several signals belong to one audience, or whether an online cluster is a real social environment.

## Unit of analysis

Prefer an `interaction episode` over an isolated post:

```text
triggering post/video/event
ordered replies and responses
participant roles at safe granularity
time and platform context
observable outcome: continuation, correction, invitation, conflict, moderation, or offline action
```

Do not treat likes or follower overlap as conversation. Preserve thread order; meaning often appears in callbacks, repair, irony, moderation, and who is allowed to speak with authority.

## Sampling

Sample deliberately across:

- at least two time windows when persistence matters;
- ordinary and high-attention content;
- creators/organizers, regular participants, newcomers, critics, and quiet or low-engagement contexts when visible;
- at least two environments or a documented access limitation;
- one adjacent group and one negative case.

Record the search/ranking route. A recommendation feed or “top” sort is not a neutral sample. Stop when new episodes no longer change the communication codebook or group-boundary decision, not after an arbitrary post count.

## Interaction coding

For each episode code:

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Speaker role | Organizer, creator, regular, newcomer, venue, brand, critic, unknown? |
| Addressee | Individual, insiders, newcomers, general public, institution? |
| Communicative action | Ask, answer, joke, invite, correct, support, challenge, display status, coordinate? |
| Form | Phrase, abbreviation, emoji, register, sound, visual response, code-switch? |
| Assumed context | What is left unexplained because insiders are expected to know it? |
| Uptake | Is the contribution answered, extended, ignored, challenged, or moderated? |
| Boundary work | How are insiders, newcomers, outsiders, competence, and cringe marked? |
| Practice link | Does talk connect to a shared activity, place, event, route, purchase, or ritual? |
| Counterreading | Could this be a generic platform convention, promotion, conflict, or imitation? |

Synthesize a `Communication Grammar`:

```text
role + situation -> communicative action -> form/style -> expected response -> social meaning
```

Keep phrases in their original language with a plain-language gloss. Mark product use as `PRODUCT`, `INTERNAL ONLY`, or `AVOID`.

## Cohesion decision

Assess six dimensions without pretending the result is a probability:

1. recurring and reciprocal interaction;
2. shared practice, ritual, place, or goal;
3. language with shared contextual meaning;
4. recognizable roles, norms, and boundaries;
5. persistence across time or situations;
6. independent evidence beyond one creator, event, or platform ranking.

Use the dimensions as an auditable rubric. One severe contradiction—such as paid coordination or no interaction outside a viral prompt—may determine the verdict even when several surface signals match.

Return exactly one primary verdict:

- `COHERENT COMMUNITY`: recurring interaction and shared practice with recognizable boundaries;
- `LOOSE CULTURAL SCENE`: shared references and venues, but weak or episodic mutual engagement;
- `FUNCTIONAL AUDIENCE`: comparable need or behavior without evidence of community;
- `SHARED TREND ONLY`: common format, style, or hashtag without durable sociality;
- `INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE`: access, breadth, recency, or contradictions prevent a responsible claim.

## Three meanings of “same audience”

- `SAME PEOPLE` requires consented first-party/platform-authorized identifiers. Never infer through face recognition, handle guessing, or cross-platform identity resolution.
- `SAME SOCIAL ENVIRONMENT` requires cohesion evidence from interaction and practice.
- `FUNCTIONAL ANALOGUE` requires a comparable social function; language and aesthetics may legitimately differ.

Always state which claim was tested and which stronger claims remain unsupported.

## Human validation

For consequential work, show a paraphrased draft to several participants, a local researcher, organizer, or domain expert. Ask:

- What is recognizable?
- What sounds like an outsider’s interpretation?
- What is obsolete, platform-specific, or ironic?
- Which groups were wrongly merged or split?
- Which expressions would feel invasive if used by a brand?

Record corrections, disagreements, and resulting codebook changes in the evidence ledger.
